The problem

One copy.
One place.
In handwriting.

Walk into most racquet clubs and the entire operation — every private, every clinic, every court hour for the next four months — lives in one book at the front desk, in pencil. It works, and it has worked for thirty years, right up to the moment someone who isn't standing at that desk needs to know something.

So they call. A pro halfway to the club asks what time he's on. A member asks whether four o'clock is open. The desk spends its morning answering a question it already answered, and the answer is always two feet away from the person who needs it.

When a booking moves, the desk erases and rewrites, and the page is immediately correct. The pro is not. He finds out at the gate, or from the member, or after he's already driven in. And if the page goes missing, or a cup of coffee lands on Tuesday, there is no second copy of anything.

  • One copyNo backup, no history, no second place to look.
  • Tens of calls a dayMost of them are someone asking what time they're on court.
  • No remote viewThe director can't see today without walking to the desk.

How it works

Three steps, and the book is on every phone.

Nobody at the club learns new software. The desk keeps writing the page exactly the way it always has.

  1. Step one

    Photograph the page

    At the end of the morning the desk holds up a phone and takes one picture of the page. That is the entire capture step — no typing, no double entry, no new habit for anyone to remember at four o'clock on a Saturday.

  2. Step two

    Verify in seconds

    Courtime reads the grid — courts, thirty-minute slots, coach names, the arrows that mean a booking runs long — and lays the parsed schedule beside your photo. Anything it is unsure of comes back flagged. You tap the flagged cells, fix what's wrong, and confirm. Nothing reaches a single phone before a person says it's right.

  3. Step three

    Every phone updates

    The moment you confirm, it's on every phone that matters. Each coach sees only their own hours — not the club's day, theirs. When something moves later, the phone that cares about it buzzes: Your 3:00 moved to Court 2.

100% verified, not 100% AI.

The model does the typing. A person does the vouching. That review step is the product, and it never gets automated away — no page has ever published itself, and none ever will.

The two surfaces

Two views, one schedule.

The desk gets a dense grid shaped like the page it already knows. The pro gets four lines and a notification. Same data, one second apart.

Reversible by design

You don't have to
give up paper.

Courtime prints the day sheet in the layout the desk already reads: same columns, same order, same shape as the book. Clip it to the board. Hand it to the pro who will never open an app. Paper stops being the only copy without ever stopping being paper.

Through the pilot, the book stays the source of truth and Courtime just mirrors it — the desk changes nothing about how it works. If it hasn't earned its place after a month, you close the tab. There's no migration to unwind, because nothing was ever taken away.

When the club is ready, the direction flips: the app becomes the record and the printed sheet becomes the copy. Most clubs never announce the day it happens. They notice a week later that nobody has opened the book.

Questions

The five things every club asks.

Do we have to stop using paper?

No. Courtime prints the day sheet in the layout your desk already reads, so the paper you like keeps existing — it just stops being the only copy. Through the pilot the book stays the source of truth and the app mirrors it. If the club later decides the app should be the record, that's a decision you make on a Monday, not a condition of starting.

How accurate is the photo import?

Accurate enough that reviewing a page takes under a minute, and it doesn't matter if it weren't, because every page is checked by a person before it publishes. The parsed grid appears beside your photo with anything uncertain flagged; you fix the flagged cells and confirm. The AI never publishes on its own — that's a design decision, not a setting.

What if we already have months booked on paper?

One archive session handles it. Someone photographs every future page of the book — about forty minutes for a hundred and sixty pages — and the batch gets imported and reviewed in an afternoon. From then on, the desk clips a sticky tab on any future page it writes on, and those pages get re-photographed weekly. You keep working exactly as you do now while the back half of the book quietly becomes digital.

Do the coaches need to install anything?

No app store, no download. It's a web app: a coach opens the link, taps add to home screen, and it behaves like any other app on the phone — about ten seconds, once. That step also switches on push, which is how the change alerts arrive.

What does it cost to start?

Nothing. The free tier is the complete calendar, forever: photo import, every coach on the mobile app, live sync, change alerts, printable day sheets. Pro is nineteen dollars a month for the whole club if you want the digests, the assistant, the analytics and the payroll export. No card to try it and no setup fee either way.
